## Soft Knee Exosuit for Gait Rehabilitation

### Project Summary

This project explores the development of a soft, pneumatic knee exosuit designed to provide active assistance during walking. The primary goal is to create a lightweight, compliant, and comfortable wearable system that can support natural knee movement without the bulk, rigidity, or alignment challenges of traditional exoskeletons. By leveraging the advantages of soft inflatable actuators, the exosuit aims to improve mobility and reduce muscular effort for individuals undergoing rehabilitation or experiencing gait impairment.

A key technical focus of the project is improving the responsiveness of pneumatic actuation through a compact, low-latency actuator design capable of delivering meaningful torque within the time constraints of human gait. The design incorporates strategies to reduce actuation volume while preserving support capabilities, enabling the exosuit to provide assistance throughout both flexion and extension phases of walking. The system is built with wearability and user comfort in mind, allowing for longer use in real-world environments.

To ensure effective and personalized assistance, the project also integrates a real-time control architecture that uses wearable sensors to estimate gait state and synchronize actuator behavior with the user’s movement. A learning-based control policy continuously adapts inflation and deflation timing to the individual’s walking pattern, enabling intuitive and responsive support. Together, these efforts aim to advance the capabilities of soft wearable robotics for mobility assistance, with applications in clinical rehabilitation and everyday functional support.
