## Scalable and Customizable Intent Inference and Motion Planning for Socially-Adept Autonomous Vehicles

### Project Summary

With an increasing number of autonomous systems developed for defense missions, it is vital to examine these systems’
ability to understand, influence, and coordinate safely and effectively with human actions. The overall objective of
this project is to explore fundamental processes of human trust in autonomy and adaptive autonomy design to calibrate
human trust in human-machine joint action (HMJA). This project addresses 1) an increasing and unmet need for humans and
machines to continuously adapt to each other and respond to evolving circumstances, 2) a need to model risksensitive
human decisions in theory of mind (ToM) and 3) a lack of knowledge in how the accuracy a machine’s ToM model impacts
team coordination and trust calibration.

This project has four goals towards understanding the relationship between the machine’s ToM and human trust in HMJA
tasks with risks and incomplete information. 1) Incorporating risks and intent inference into the machine's ToM for
HMJA. Here, “joint action” refers to tasks where a human and machine agent continuously adapt and coordinate online to
achieve shared goals. Such tasks require the machine (e.g., Skyborg) to build a ToM to infer human belief and predict
their actions. Instead of assuming the human actions are noisy optimal, we integrate game theory, cumulative prospect
theory, and decision field theory to consider mutual inference and risky decision making in HMJA. 2) Understanding how
machine’s ToM impacts human trust. We will evaluate the machine’s trustworthiness in HMJA with the proposed ToM, and how
the trustworthiness varies across tasks, machine’s strategies, and individuals. 3) Handling failures of ToM for trust
calibration. The machine’s ToM may attribute human belief incorrectly and generate confusing machine actions, and we
propose to facilitate trust calibration by adjusting the machine’s roles online and explaining its ToM to human offline
upon such failures. We will evaluate the new ToM model in a human-machine joint pursuit game with thirty humans and test
three hypotheses using self-report as well as behavioral and psychological measures.

This project will lead to new discoveries in ToM and trust processes in HMJA. With the ultimate goal of trust
calibration, it will enable machines to model human actions in the presence of risks and incomplete information,
resulting in safe, transparent, and efficient coordination with humans in various defense missions as well as accelerate
adoption of robot/AI technologies
